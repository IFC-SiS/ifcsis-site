How Curation Works

Curators recommend; parents decide

4 November 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

The standard never decides what a child should see. That decision belongs to the parent, and most parents will not want to study a vocabulary to make it. Curation lets a parent hand the detail to an organization they already trust, without handing over the decision.

What a curator is

A curator is any organization parents trust to make these choices well: a parents' or grandparents' association, a school, a library, a congregation, a children's health group. Curators need no special status. Their authority is the trust of the families who follow them.

What a curator publishes

A curator publishes a list: for each descriptor and each age, whether content is allowed, held for the parent to review, or blocked. A list may also define terms in the shared vocabulary, such as a garment by the anatomical regions it covers. Curators choose; they never add labels to content. The description stays the same for everyone.

How a parent uses it

The parent subscribes each child to a list, or to several. Every line is visible, and the parent can change any of them, for any child. Siblings can follow different lists, and settings follow each child's enrollment, not the household. When two lists disagree, the stricter applies unless the parent decides otherwise.

Why lists are public

Anyone can read what a curator allows and blocks, so a curator cannot quietly drift. A parent who disagrees can switch lists at any time, which keeps curators answerable to families. Lists are versioned, so a parent can see what changed and when.

The familiar model

Shoppers already work this way. Several certifying agencies read the same ingredient facts, apply their own rules, and the shopper follows the one they trust; no shopper has to master the rules. Curation brings that model to children's content: one set of facts, many trusted judgments.

The test

A progressive parent and a conservative parent can both use the same metadata to make opposite choices, without either feeling the labels were written for the other one.

The standard describes. Curators recommend. Parents decide.
