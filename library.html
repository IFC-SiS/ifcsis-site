WP-05 · One-page brief

The Geography of Zero

Physical distance never acted alone. It generated a bundle of frictions — travel, attendance, search, coordination, translation, temporal access, visibility — that mattered because they interacted, and networked technologies reduce them together. Some outcomes those frictions once supplied are still wanted after they fall away. This paper proposes three tests for identifying which, and for deciding whether they can be supplied at another institutional layer without restoring the cost.

It is the theoretical root the rest of the series assumes.

Attractive nuisance does not govern platforms. What it supplies is a principle, and a precedent: how a vague duty of reasonable care became a specific requirement — a fence — once a standards body wrote it down and governments adopted it by reference. The same three steps are available for platforms where adults reach children.

WP-02 · One-page brief

Refusal Is Not Referral

AI systems now occupy relational roles with children — tutor, confidant, first responder to a disclosure. Roles of that kind have always carried professional duties. Declining to answer is not the same as getting a child to someone who can help, and current deployments do not meet the standard the role implies.

WP-03 · One-page brief

The Misallocated Burden

A least-cost-avoider analysis of child-safety cost allocation. Who is best placed to prevent the harm, who is actually bearing the cost of preventing it, and what follows when those are not the same party. The answer is not that anyone is acting in bad faith; it is that the burden has settled in the wrong place.

WP-04 · One-page brief

The Demand Side

An identification problem. Demand for child-safety provision has been inferred from instruments that move three variables at the same time, and the confound has never been isolated. The paper argues for what would have to be measured to know, and is careful not to convert that reasoning into a prediction.

NB-01 · One-page brief

Siting the School

A school has a registrar and an address. Enrollment asks who may enter; siting asks what a child crosses to get there. Age assurance is the registrar and says nothing about the environment. Where content was never admitted, there is nothing inside to circumvent — a property of construction rather than a claim about detection accuracy.

The standard — descriptors

How the vocabulary is written, and why it is descriptive rather than evaluative. Start at the standard itself; the notes below cover each part in turn. Contact is governed by enrollment rather than by content rules, and is set out in Enrollment Before Contact. The machine-readable schema is published at standard.ifcsis.org.

C-SiS · The standard

C-SiS: The Child-Safe Internet Standard

Content labels online are usually ratings: moral judgments that do not translate across faiths, cultures or languages. C-SiS takes the opposite approach — it describes, and does not judge. The hub page sets out the format, how it is adopted, and links to each part of the vocabulary.

The descriptor is the standard; the country and the parent set the limit.

C-SiS · New · Descriptor note

Images

Describe the anatomy, not the garment. “Nudity” is too large a word, so it breaks into anatomical regions, each with one observable coverage state. Two people looking at the same image would assign the same values without sharing any beliefs.

C-SiS · New · Descriptor note

Subject Matter

Describe the act, not the verdict. Violence is recorded by its physical facts; legal labels such as murder or self-defence are conclusions about intent, so they stay with the country. Every subject records how it appears — mentioned, discussed or depicted.

Read the note →

C-SiS · New · Descriptor note

How Curation Works

The standard never decides what a child should see. Curators publish open lists of what they allow at which ages; the parent accepts a list or changes any line of it, for any child. One set of facts, many trusted judgments.

Why the full papers are sent rather than posted

The longer documents carry material that is specific to a jurisdiction, a sector or an institution — and in some cases organisational proposals that only make sense once we know who is reading. Rather than post a version written for nobody in particular, we send the one that fits. Tell us the audience and the question in the request and we will reply with the relevant document, usually within a few days. Requests are welcome from critics as readily as from allies.
