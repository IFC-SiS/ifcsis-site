Safety by admission,
not by filtering.

The International Foundation for Child-Safe internet Standards develops the metadata standard and admission criteria for a governed namespace — a space designed so that harmful material is absent because it was never admitted, rather than removed after it appears.

Read the architecture → How the standard works
The claim

Every tool we have operates after the harm.

Filtering, blocking, moderation and takedown all act after publication — after a child has already been reached. They are treatments applied downstream of a supply that is universal, unlicensed and unbounded.

An admission-based namespace works differently. Publishers apply; published criteria are applied before an address is issued. Admission is a tenancy: accept the terms or do not enter, and breach the terms and lose the address. Nothing is inspected in transit, because nothing needs to be.

An independent standard

One canonical, machine-readable metadata schema, published openly and verifiable by anyone.

Uniform structure, local content

The format is invariant everywhere; the thresholds inside it are set by each jurisdiction.

Voluntary at every layer

A household election, never a mandate. The open internet continues unchanged alongside it.

A published standard, applied at the address.

Land-use law has governed what may be built where for a century, through criteria published in advance and applied at the point of permission. The addressing layer works the same way: addresses are issued under agreement, conditioned, recorded, and withdrawn when the terms are breached.

01 · The problem

The internet eliminated the protective friction of distance.

Children have always been protected in part by geography — libraries, schools, cinemas and shops have physical edges, and those edges did real protective work. Point-to-point addressing has no edges: schoolwork and a self-harm forum sit the same distance apart, one reach. Exposure no longer requires seeking; it requires only adjacency.

The prevailing model treats the internet as a river to be treated downstream — but the upstream is universal, there is no chokepoint, and treatment always acts after exposure. A block list is a record of harms already discovered.

02 · Admission, not filtering

The classification decision happens before the content gets an address.

A governed namespace is additive, not subtractive: it is built by admitting vetted material into a bounded space, not by filtering harmful material out of an open one. Nothing is inspected in transit and no traffic is intercepted — the architecture operates on a different layer entirely from the one encryption protects. That produces a defined, verifiable boundary other systems can bind to.

What follows describes a model, not a service in operation.

Admission

Governs who enters. A publisher applies, identity and purpose are verified, published criteria are applied, and a registry agreement is signed as a condition of the address.

Conformity

Governs who remains. Content changes after admission, so review is continuous — failure returns the registrant to the admission question. Either gate alone is a label.

Enforcement runs in two directions: vertically, registry to registrant — admission required accepting the terms, so breach permits rapid suspension without litigation; and horizontally, registrant to registrant — every participant enters on the same published terms and so has standing against the others.

Honest limits: coverage is not integrity

The standard governs what is inside the district — it does not claim to prevent a determined adolescent from leaving it. A child using a VPN is outside the namespace, not leaking through it. That is a coverage boundary, not a system failure.

03 · Why this is not censorship

Six design conditions, and all six are load-bearing.

An architecture that can admit can also exclude. What distinguishes this from a coercive scheme is structure, not intent — replicating the technology without these conditions produces a different system entirely.

1. Compulsion

Elected by the household, never mandated.

2. Authorship

The state does not author the code; an independent body does.

3. Externality

No third party is compelled to suppress speech outside the namespace.

4. Substitution

The open internet continues unchanged, in parallel, in full.

5. Governance

Policy authority is independent, with published criteria and appeal.

6. Exit

A household may discontinue at any time, without penalty.

04 · The open questions

Three questions any such design has to answer.

Earlier efforts to set aside a safer part of the namespace — kids.us, .xxx, .kids — were serious attempts by serious people, and the public record of each is worth reading. Between them they leave three questions open.

Authority — on what basis the rules of the space are set. Distribution — how the space is actually reached. Verification — how conformity is checked after entry, and by whom. The argument here is that the three have to be answered together.

The metadata standard

One format. Many jurisdictions.

The foundation maintains a single canonical metadata schema and ratifies changes to it. National trials propose refinements upward; the foundation resolves them into one version — the discipline that prevents every national effort from becoming an unrelated experiment.

View the published schema →

"Uniform" is a technical claim, not a content claim.

The schema is invariant — fields, structure, semantics, the encoding of an admission decision — in every jurisdiction that adopts it. The values inside those fields are local: what counts as suitable for an age band, what the admission floor requires, stays domestic content judgment.

The analogy is ISO 8601. The date format is identical everywhere on earth; what is scheduled on that date is entirely local. The foundation owns the format — the nation owns the policy applied through it.

Pluralism lives above the floor.

The admission code is an objective minimum. Above it, families layer higher standards through third-party curators according to their own values — a faith-based curator, an educational curator, a values-based curator can all operate on the same namespace without fragmenting the compliance burden, and without the schema itself classifying content by any protected characteristic.

Who decides what, in the model

Roles the model separates. Nothing here describes a body in operation.

The policy body — defines the jurisdictional admission floor and maintains the code.
The accredited reviewer — makes substantive conformity judgments under that code.
The registry — records operative status and executes remedies; it does not judge substance.
An independent appellate function — hears contested findings, separate from the body that made them.
About & governance

An independent standards body.

The International Foundation for Child-Safe internet Standards maintains the metadata schema and the model admission code for governed, child-safe namespaces. It does not operate a registry, does not make substantive conformity judgments about individual material, and does not set any jurisdiction's content policy.

That separation is deliberate and structural: the clearest lesson from earlier attempts is that a policy body which depends on a registry operator eventually serves it.

Governance

The schema is published openly so conformance can be checked by anyone — including by people who disagree with it. Policy authority sits with the foundation rather than with any registry operator, and the criteria are written down so they can be read, compared and contested.

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Isn't this just censorship with extra steps?

No: participation is elected by the household, never mandated; the open internet continues unchanged in parallel; nothing is taken down anywhere; and governance is designed to be independent, with published criteria and a right of appeal. Remove any one condition and it becomes a different, coercive system — which is exactly why all six are written down.

What about a VPN, or a determined teenager?

The standard governs what is inside the namespace; it does not claim to prevent someone from leaving it. That's a coverage boundary, not a system failure — a library's collection policy doesn't fail because a child borrows a book from a friend.

Why not just require age verification?

Age verification asks "how old is this person?" at every gate on the open web — expensive, privacy-invasive, and it still doesn't tell you what a space's rules are. Admission asks "what are the rules of this space?" once, at entry, and lets a publisher choose to meet them.

Who sets the content thresholds?

Each jurisdiction does, for itself. The foundation standardises the format the decision is expressed in — the same discipline ISO 8601 applies to dates — not what the decision says.

What stops a government from abusing the criteria?

Nothing about the format guarantees justice on its own — but published, machine-readable criteria are visible, comparable across jurisdictions, and quotable by anyone who wants to contest them. An exclusion written down is easier to challenge than one that never was.

WP-05 · One-page brief

The Geography of Zero

Physical distance never acted alone. It generated a bundle of frictions — travel, attendance, search, coordination, translation, temporal access, visibility — that mattered because they interacted, and networked technologies reduce them together. Some outcomes those frictions once supplied are still wanted after they fall away. This paper proposes three tests for identifying which, and for deciding whether they can be supplied at another institutional layer without restoring the cost.

It is the theoretical root the rest of the series assumes.

The standard — descriptors

How the vocabulary is written, and why it is descriptive rather than evaluative. Start at the standard itself; the notes below cover each part in turn. Contact is governed by enrollment rather than by content rules, and is set out in Enrollment Before Contact. The machine-readable schema is published at standard.ifcsis.org.

C-SiS · The standard

C-SiS: The Child-Safe Internet Standard

Content labels online are usually ratings: moral judgments that do not translate across faiths, cultures or languages. C-SiS takes the opposite approach — it describes, and does not judge. The hub page sets out the format, how it is adopted, and links to each part of the vocabulary.

The descriptor is the standard; the country and the parent set the limit.

C-SiS · New · Descriptor note

Images

Describe the anatomy, not the garment. “Nudity” is too large a word, so it breaks into anatomical regions, each with one observable coverage state. Two people looking at the same image would assign the same values without sharing any beliefs.

C-SiS · New · Descriptor note

Subject Matter

Describe the act, not the verdict. Violence is recorded by its physical facts; legal labels such as murder or self-defence are conclusions about intent, so they stay with the country. Every subject records how it appears — mentioned, discussed or depicted.

C-SiS · New · Descriptor note

How Curation Works

The standard never decides what a child should see. Curators publish open lists of what they allow at which ages; the parent accepts a list or changes any line of it, for any child. One set of facts, many trusted judgments.

Why the full papers are sent rather than posted

The longer documents carry material that is specific to a jurisdiction, a sector or an institution — and in some cases organisational proposals that only make sense once we know who is reading. Rather than post a version written for nobody in particular, we send the one that fits.
