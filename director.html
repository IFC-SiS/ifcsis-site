Director

S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

Director, International Foundation for Child-Safe internet Standards

The work

S. Harrison Knudson is Director of the International Foundation for Child-Safe internet Standards, an independent standards body that maintains the metadata schema and the model admission code for governed, child-safe namespaces. The Foundation does not operate a registry, does not make substantive conformity judgments about individual material, and does not set any jurisdiction's content policy.

His work applies land-use and planning law — the legal technology of admission, placement and conformity — to internet governance. The organising claim is that the domain address is the one feature of the internet structurally analogous to geographic space, which is what makes governance at the point of admission possible at all. Where a filtering regime inspects material after it has been published and addressed, an admission regime asks a prior question: what may be present here in the first place. Content that was never admitted leaves nothing inside to circumvent, which is a property of construction rather than a claim about detection accuracy.

Working papers

He is the author of the Foundation's working-paper series on additive namespace architecture, which runs to four papers and a set of shorter notes. Zoning the Namespace sets out the theoretical root the others assume. The Misallocated Burden treats cost allocation, asking which party is best placed to avoid the harm at least cost. The Demand Side argues that demand for child online safety has been inferred from markets in safety instruments, and that instrument markets measure something else. Refusal Is Not Referral concerns the duties that attach to systems occupying sustained relational roles with children.

Shorter notes address adjacent questions: where a child-safe environment is sited and who may occupy it, what qualifies a system to work inside one, and what a national trial would have to produce to be falsifiable.

The series is listed at the library, where one-page summaries are readable on the site and full papers are sent on request. The metadata schema is published openly at standard.ifcsis.org so that conformance can be checked by anyone, including by people who disagree with it.

Approach

The Foundation's separation from registry operation is deliberate and structural. The clearest lesson from earlier attempts at namespace-level governance is that a policy body which depends on a registry operator eventually serves it. Policy authority accordingly sits with the Foundation rather than with any operator, and the criteria are written down so they can be read, compared and contested.

Nothing in the architecture compels participation. An organisation joining a governed namespace keeps everything it already has and adds a separately addressed presence inside; a household electing into one makes a single decision rather than configuring settings service by service. The design removes burdens rather than adding them, and it sits alongside other regulatory approaches without depending on any of them.
