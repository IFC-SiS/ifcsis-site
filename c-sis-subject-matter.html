C-SiS · Descriptor note

Subject Matter

Describe the act, not the verdict

4 November 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

Images are one kind of content. Subject matter is harder, because the usual labels are verdicts. C-SiS describes what a work contains and how it treats it, using vocabularies that already exist.

Sources that already exist

Librarians and publishers describe what a work is about without judging it: library subject headings and the book trade's BISAC codes already include themes such as self-harm and body image. Criminal codes and forensic medicine describe physical acts precisely. Film and game ratings contribute categories, but not their age verdicts or intensity words such as “mild” or “strong”.

Describe the act, not the verdict

Violence is recorded by its physical facts: mechanism (blunt force, sharp force, firearm), region injured, and whether the wound is visible. Legal labels such as murder or self-defence are conclusions about intent, so they stay with the country. Slapstick and combat sport are violence, labelled distinctly so curators can decide.

Motive as presented

Content can present a reason for an act: self-defence, gain, intimidation of a group, or unclear and contested. C-SiS records the motive as the work presents it, never as a finding of fact, and uses descriptive phrases rather than politically contested labels.

Treatment

Every subject records how it appears: mentioned, discussed or depicted. A recovery story and a how-to page may share a theme; treatment tells them apart.

Themes

Self-harm, body image, substance use and gambling each get descriptors of the same precision. Games of chance are gambling, whoever benefits.

Belief: described as subject matter; instruction follows enrollment

Belief as subject matter is described strictly and neutrally: the topic a work addresses, such as the nature of God or the resurrection, and how it presents it, as held, as one view among several, or critically. C-SiS never ranks or labels beliefs. Faith as instruction follows enrollment: the parent chooses the congregation, faith school or teacher, as with any other educator.

The descriptor is the standard; the country and the parent set the limit.

This note may be read and circulated freely. Critical reactions — doctrinal, technical or empirical — are more useful than supportive ones.
