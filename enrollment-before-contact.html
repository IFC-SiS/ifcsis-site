Enrollment Before Contact

Who may form a relationship with a child online

4 November 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

Every offline institution that serves a child begins the same way: a parent hands the child over. School, camp, a doctor, a coach, a tutor all start with enrollment. The internet is the only place a stranger can form a relationship with a child without a parent ever being asked. It is illegal everywhere except the internet.

Enrollment is the credential

Platforms do not need to guess who is a child. They need to stop forming relationships with children unilaterally. Enrollment restores the offline default: the parent's act, not the platform's guess, is what identifies the child and opens the relationship. Self-declared age never qualifies.

The parent holds the account

The parent or legal guardian is the account holder; a child never is. The child uses the line, and the parent owns it. Contact with a child's account is closed by default and opens only by a grant the parent can revoke. Records that are revoked, expired or unverifiable fail closed.

Contact runs through the enrolling organization

No adult may contact a child unless both belong to an organization in which the child is enrolled, such as a school, club or congregation. Contact between children runs inside that system, between enrolled members, on rules the enrolling organization sets and the parent accepted at enrollment. Same class, same grade and same area are descriptors like any other, so each organization draws its own lines in the same terms.

AI enters through enrollment, not around it

An AI that speaks to a child takes on a relationship with that child. If it does not know who the parent is, it should not address the child. Enrolled, it inherits the norms of the profession whose role it fills, such as teaching or tutoring, including the same duties to report harm, and questions outside that role go back to the parent. AI should join the networks that already serve children, not bypass them.

What enrollment does for enforcement

Any adult who takes on a relationship with a child owes that child a duty, and in the real world we make that relationship begin with a parent's consent: enrollment. For liability, enrollment sets the duty at the start instead of leaving courts to infer it after harm. For consumer protection, enrollment turns vague disclaimers into representations an attorney general can audit. Enrollment doesn't replace either enforcement track; it gives both something firm to stand on.

Enrollment is neither a ban nor a promise to make platforms safe after contact. It decides who may form a relationship with a child, before contact.
