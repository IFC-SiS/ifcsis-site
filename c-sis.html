C-SiS: The Child-Safe Internet Standard

Descriptive terms for images and subject matter; enrollment for contact

4 November 2026, revised 19 November 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

Content labels online are usually ratings: moral judgments that do not translate across faiths, cultures or languages. C-SiS takes the opposite approach. It describes; it does not judge. The descriptor is the standard; the limit is set by the country and the parent.

Images: describe the anatomy, not the garment →

Images are described in international anatomical terms, region by region, with an observable coverage state. Garments are never named.

Subject matter: describe the act, not the verdict →

Themes use vocabularies that already exist among librarians, publishers and criminal codes, with treatment and motive recorded as the content presents them.

Contact: enrollment governs →

Who may contact a child is governed by enrollment, not by content rules.

Curators recommend; parents decide →

Organizations parents trust publish open lists of what they allow at which ages; the parent accepts a list or customizes it for each child.

The format is universal; the values are local

Jurisdictions set their own age gates as values: one may choose 16, another 18, while the descriptors stay identical. The child's jurisdiction applies, and a parent may always choose stricter.

Codes, not words

Every descriptor carries a stable code that a computer reads the same way in every country. The words people see are labels on that code, in whatever language they speak; a label in Arabic, Spanish or Hindi points to the same code as the label in English. Translation changes the label, never the descriptor. Where an international vocabulary already assigns identifiers, as anatomy and library subject headings do, C-SiS anchors its codes to them.

Built to be adopted by reference

C-SiS is designed as a readable code, written in MUST, SHOULD and MAY, that nations, platforms and curators can adopt in full, in part or with additions, the way jurisdictions adopt a model building code. To be adopted by a legislature or an agency, a technical standard has to meet tests that law already applies. C-SiS is built to meet them:

  • Machine-readable. Every descriptor has a language-independent code.
  • Descriptive. Terms come from established scientific, medical and technical vocabularies and describe what can be observed. No term is a verdict.
  • Neutral. The standard is neutral as to religion, belief and viewpoint. It never ranks or labels a belief.
  • Open. It is developed and maintained through an open consensus process.
  • Free. Every edition is free to read, for everyone.

A conformance core cannot be removed, and conformance is to be verified by public tests in many languages.

How the standard is made

Anyone may take part: families, safeguarding practitioners, educators, librarians, operators and independent experts. No single interest may dominate. Every comment receives a written response, the disposition of each objection is published, and anyone whose objection is rejected may appeal. A term is adopted when the participants reach consensus, not when IFC-SiS decides.

Editions

C-SiS is published in numbered, dated editions. A law or rule adopts a specific edition, and a later edition binds no one until the adopting body adopts it through its own process. Every edition, including every superseded one, stays free to read in a permanent public archive. The vocabulary grows more precise as it is used: terms are refined through the same public process, and replaced terms are mapped forward to their successors, never deleted, so a code adopted in one edition can always be traced into the next.

Current status: working draft toward Edition 1.0. The descriptor notes on this site are the draft text, and comments on them are part of the process.
