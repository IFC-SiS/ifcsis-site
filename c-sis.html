C-SiS: The Child-Safe Internet Standard

Descriptive terms for images and subject matter; enrollment for contact

4 November 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

Content labels online are usually ratings: moral judgments that do not translate across faiths, cultures or languages. C-SiS takes the opposite approach. It describes; it does not judge. The descriptor is the standard; the limit is set by the country and the parent.

Images: describe the anatomy, not the garment →

Images are described in international anatomical terms, region by region, with an observable coverage state. Garments are never named.

Subject matter: describe the act, not the verdict →

Themes use vocabularies that already exist among librarians, publishers and criminal codes, with treatment and motive recorded as the content presents them.

Contact: enrollment governs →

Who may contact a child is governed by enrollment, not by content rules.

Curators recommend; parents decide →

Organizations parents trust publish open lists of what they allow at which ages; the parent accepts a list or customizes it for each child.

The format is universal; the values are local

Jurisdictions set their own age gates as values: one may choose 16, another 18, while the descriptors stay identical. The child's jurisdiction applies, and a parent may always choose stricter.

Adopted, not owned; precise through use

C-SiS is designed as a readable code, written in MUST, SHOULD and MAY, that nations, platforms and curators can adopt in full, in part or with additions, the way jurisdictions adopt a model building code. A conformance core cannot be removed, and conformance is to be verified by public tests in many languages. The vocabulary is versioned and grows more precise as it is used: terms are refined through a public process, and replaced terms are mapped forward, never deleted.

Maintained by a foundation, published for everyone, owned by no government.

This note may be read and circulated freely. Critical reactions — terminological, technical or practical — are more useful than supportive ones.
